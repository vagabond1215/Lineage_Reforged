# Chapter 6

The divine blaze around Sehir lasted only a few breaths more.

Then it thinned into drifting sparks and left him in darkness, blood, and falling stone.

He moved anyway.

What remained of the chamber collapsed in waves, each impact loosening the next. Sehir forced a path through dust and splintered timber, hauling himself through gaps that should have been too narrow. The strength that had lifted stone above his head was fading fast, but it left a sharp, foreign clarity behind - as if a new law had been carved directly into his marrow.

By the time he broke through a split service tunnel and reached the upper galleries, his lungs burned and his limbs felt hollow.

Alive was all he had.

Outside, dusk had gone iron-gray.

Blackstone Camp lay half-buried in mine dust where the cave-in had vented through old shafts. A section of hoist frame had collapsed near the main mouth. Beyond it, against a cracked ore cart, Arienne sat hunched with one hand pressed to her chest, her staff lying in the mud.

She had overdrawn her mana.

Every breath shook her shoulders.

Sehir crossed the yard without speaking.

She sensed him too late.

Arienne looked up, startled, and reached for a blade no longer at her belt. Sehir caught her by the throat and pinned her against the cart frame before she could finish the motion.

A weak spark jumped from her fingers and died.

"You should be dead," she rasped.

"So should Aldren," Sehir said.

The words were still in the air when the new power answered.

White fire erupted around his hand, bright enough to carve the world into shadow and outline. Arienne cried out and twisted, but the light wrapped her neck in a ring of burning script that neither of them could read and both of them understood.

For three heartbeats it was blinding.

On the fourth, it sank beneath her skin like molten silver drawn into glass.

When the light vanished, a pale mark remained at her throat - a narrow choker of linked sigils.

Arienne froze.

Fear came first, then rage, then panic as she felt something deeper moving through her mind. She clenched both fists, fighting for purchase inside herself.

"What did you do to me?"

Sehir did not know the formula ahead of time, yet he spoke it with absolute certainty.

"I executed judgment. For attempted murder, conspiracy, and treason, I place you under punitive servitude until restitution is complete. You will not raise violence against me, against my lawful command, or against the purpose of this sentence."

The choker mark flashed once.

Arienne doubled over with a choking gasp.

Sehir felt the mechanism lock in place. Mutual contracts required consent from both sides. Punitive contracts did not. In unilateral execution, the offended party could petition for severity, but the executor held final authority. Terms could be narrowed, expanded, or denied, so long as the sentence resolved as just and equivalent under the weight of the offense.

The magic did not stop at obedience.

It pushed on thought.

It pulled instinct away from violence, chaos, and self-justified cruelty, and toward restitution. Over time, it would not only prevent betrayal - it would wear down the impulses that made betrayal feel reasonable.

Arienne felt that pressure immediately.

Her face moved through anger, terror, and defiance before flattening into an unnatural calm that frightened her more than the pain had.

"No," she whispered. "No, this isn't right."

"You may contest terms," Sehir said. "Speak."

She swallowed hard. "Reduce duration."

"Denied."

The mark pulsed, and she flinched.

"Then define service," she said through her teeth. "If this is judgment, define it."

"You will answer questions truthfully. You will work under my command to repair the harm you helped cause. You will preserve life where you once took it."

Arienne shut her eyes.

When she opened them, the hatred was still there, but it had lost control of her hands.

Sehir released her.

She did not run.

They returned to the barracks in silence.

Night settled over Blackstone while Sehir scrubbed blood from his hands and spread Aldren's surviving papers across the table. Arienne sat opposite him, wrists unbound, posture rigid, the pale contract mark surfacing whenever the lantern flame bent her way.

The interrogation lasted for hours.

"Name the queen's internal network."

"The Quiet Ledger."

"Primary handlers?"

"Marcell Durn in treasury procurement. Lady Hest Varin in the roads office. Captain Ivo Seln in internal courier review."

"Methods?"

"Infrastructure inflation, false bid routing, coercive debt contracts, staged raids, engineered accidents."

"Assassination directives?"

Her jaw tightened. The mark glimmered.

"Yes. Targets removed under bandit cover, monster incidents, collapse events, or prison transfer paperwork."

"How many were tied to this mission?"

"I don't know every name. I know the queen approved it personally."

"Was the king involved?"

Arienne met his eyes for the first time since the binding.

"No. He is excluded from covert channels. He receives edited summaries after the fact."

Sehir absorbed that in silence.

It was not relief.

Only a colder equation.

He pressed on through dead-drop locations, cipher variants, port contacts, and coin routes. Every truthful answer cost her in visible tension - not physical pain, but friction between who she had been and what the contract was forcing her to become.

At last Sehir's vision started to blur from exhaustion and blood loss.

"Enough," he said.

Arienne looked genuinely startled.

"Sleep," he told her. "That is an order."

The contract accepted it before she could resist.

Her body slackened, and she folded onto the far bedroll within minutes.

Sehir remained awake a while longer, watching the lantern flame and listening to the mountain settle in the distance.

When sleep finally came, it came dreamless.

They woke at an hour that belonged to neither night nor morning, when the sky was still black but no longer deep.

Sehir stood first, every wound stiff and hot.

Arienne watched him from her bedroll, guarded.

"Are we going back to the capital?"

"No."

He started packing.

"If I return now, the queen controls the story before I reach the city gates. She controls the roads, the couriers, and the witnesses."

Arienne said nothing.

Sehir tightened a strap and kept speaking, half to her and half to the plan forming in his own mind.

"Westfall is overburdened. Blackstone needs labor, security, and infrastructure."

He looked up.

"I'll contract violent repeat offenders into indentured restitution. They leave isolation, work under enforceable terms, and earn sentence reduction through compliance."

Arienne narrowed her eyes. "You mean to build a labor corps out of prisoners."

"I mean to convert risk into repair," Sehir said. "And each lawful contract returns a trace of energy to the goddess."

He could feel it now - a faint warmth where divine power had burned through him in the mine.

Not recovery.

A beginning.

"Get your gear," he said.

Arienne rose without protest, and the mark at her throat flashed once in the dark.

By first light, they were already on the road back to Westfall Fortress.
