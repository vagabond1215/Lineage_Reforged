## 1. Purpose

Determine whether the accepted `Version 0.6.5 - Item, Material, And Recipe Static Content Expansion` represents a reasonably developed regional and continent-scale medieval-fantasy production system.

This audit evaluates breadth by production-chain coverage rather than recipe count alone. It distinguishes:

- completion of the exact `0.6.5` batch;
- static transformation authority;
- wider item and source catalogs that are not yet classified as craftable or noncraftable;
- missing gathering, inventory, work-order, quality, loss, repair, and market owners;
- the smallest later evidence program needed before another recipe expansion.

This document does not authorize new recipes, quantities, resources, commodities, tools, workplaces, runtime commands, or economy behavior.

## 3. What `0.6.5` Actually Completed

The accepted package added 16 recipes to the prior 12, increasing the catalog:

- from 12 to 28 recipes;
- from 8 to 10 represented families.

The package established that each recipe can own a complete, explicit, game-scale batch transformation with closed references to items, tools, workplaces, skills, production chains, and market values.

It also preserved these crucial boundaries:

- production chains are descriptive macro context and do not supply recipe quantities by inheritance;
- selected quantities are `bounded_design_inference`, not universal historical yields or runtime balance;
- unsupported pastry-dough and savory-meat-pie rows were removed rather than guessed;
- no engine or application executes recipes;
- no inventory, economy, work-order, quality, loss, repair, learning, or save behavior was added.

Therefore `0.6.5` is a successful static authority and coverage seed. It is not a completed crafting or production system.

## 4. Maturity Finding

Decision:

`BATCH_COMPLETE_PRODUCTION_MATURITY_NOT_REACHED`

The current catalog is not yet reasonably complete for a regionally diverse medieval-fantasy world.

The principal mismatch is not merely that 28 is a small number. It is that the world contains 1,372 item identities, 121 macro production chains, 58 workplaces, 131 tool identities, extensive flora/fauna/mineral source topology, and continent-scale regional economies, but only a small subset has an accepted static transformation path or an explicit noncraftable classification.

A mature system does not require every item to be craftable. It does require the repository to state deliberately whether important item families are:

- directly gathered or extracted;
- refined from sources;
- assembled or crafted;
- institution-made;
- imported or trade-only;
- loot-only;
- unique, relic, or quest-owned;
- naturally occurring;
- service-produced;
- or intentionally unavailable to ordinary crafting.

Without that classification, missing recipes are indistinguishable from intentional noncraftability.

## 5. Coverage Matrix Findings

### 5.1 Acquisition and source-to-portable-form coverage

The repository has rich flora, fauna, mineral, habitat, and source-output relationships, but no general gathering, hunting, fishing, quarrying, mining, butchering, or harvesting command owner.

A later production program must preserve the distinction among:

- biological or geological source;
- harvested or extracted portable form;
- sorted, cleaned, dressed, or stabilized material;
- refined material;
- component;
- finished good;
- residue, coproduct, waste, and environmental consequence.

Static recipes should not silently create source extraction authority.

### 5.2 Primary refinement coverage

A continent-scale economy needs broad but selective representation of processes such as:

- milling and grain preparation;
- drying, smoking, salting, fermenting, and pickling;
- tanning and leather preparation;
- retting, spinning, weaving, and cloth finishing;
- ore dressing, smelting, alloying, and stock preparation;
- clay preparation, firing, pottery, brick, and tile;
- timber conversion, seasoning, sawing, and joinery stock;
- stone dressing, lime, mortar, and construction feedstocks;
- oil, wax, resin, pitch, dye, ink, soap, and similar household or specialist materials.

The current package proves some bounded transformations but does not establish systematic refinement coverage across these families.

### 5.3 Intermediate and component coverage

Mature production chains require intentional intermediate identities where they matter, such as:

- thread, yarn, cloth, cordage, leather panels, straps, rivets, rings, handles, shafts, staves, boards, fittings, blanks, ingots, flour, doughs, preserved ingredients, glazes, binders, and containers.

Intermediates should be added only when they have repeated consumers or clear trade value. The catalog should avoid one-use component noise.

### 5.4 Finished-goods coverage

The item catalog includes large tool, weapon, armor, clothing, accessory, consumable, vehicle, and commodity families. The current recipe set does not yet demonstrate representative production coverage for each important family.

Weapon and armor profile collections are not live, which is a direct precondition for richer equipment production authority. Recipe expansion should not outrun those profile and item-instance boundaries.

### 5.5 Food and regional cuisine coverage

A regionally mature food lane should cover more than generic meals. It should distinguish:

- staple grains, roots, legumes, fruits, dairy, fish, meat, and preserved foods;
- household, village, urban-specialist, institutional, military, maritime, caravan, and elite production scales;
- climate and storage constraints;
- regional ingredients and methods;
- containers and transport forms;
- feast, ration, travel, emergency, and ordinary household roles.

The current 28-recipe catalog is a useful seed, but not a convincing continent-scale cuisine system.

### 5.6 Repair, maintenance, salvage, and reuse

A medieval production economy depends heavily on maintenance. The current static recipe authority does not yet provide a general classification for:

- sharpening;
- patching;
- reforging or rehafting;
- replacing handles, straps, fittings, or containers;
- reclaiming metal, cloth, leather, timber, glass, ceramic, and organic residues;
- reworking flawed or damaged outputs.

These should remain separate from initial manufacture where ownership, loss, quality, and item-instance state differ.

### 5.7 Regional and institutional specialization

The accepted production synthesis distinguishes household, village/town, urban-specialist, institutional, elite/strategic, and rare/exceptional access bands.

The current recipe catalog does not yet prove that each continent or major region has differentiated production strengths, imports, constraints, guild or institutional dependencies, and fallback technologies.

Magic must remain an explicitly constrained alternative. It cannot replace ordinary technology merely because a spell, crystal, affinity, or magical institution exists.

## 6. Runtime And Authority Gaps

The static recipe catalog must not be mistaken for executable crafting.

A later runtime package still requires explicit owners for:

- recipe knowledge, access, and learning;
- item-instance inputs and reservations;
- tool possession, condition, wear, and substitution;
- workplace presence, capacity, maintenance, and access;
- labor, time, energy, fuel, water, and environmental requirements;
- quality, defects, loss, waste, residues, and byproducts;
- interruption, cancellation, and recovery;
- inventory mutation and output placement;
- market availability and pricing;
- command, result, event, receipt, persistence, replay, correction, and UI behavior.

No recipe-count expansion can close those owners by inference.

## 7. Reasonable Regional And Continental Completion Standard

Static production coverage should be considered reasonably mature when:

1. Every major item family has a deliberate acquisition/production/noncraftable classification.
2. Important production families have representative source, refinement, intermediate, component, finished-good, maintenance, and storage coverage where applicable.
3. Regional economies have explicit strengths, dependencies, imported goods, and culturally distinct outputs.
4. Household, communal, specialist, institutional, elite, and rare production scales are distinguished without implying universal access.
5. Resources, commodities, items, recipes, chains, workplaces, tools, skills, and market values have closed references and non-overlapping ownership.
6. Recipe quantities remain bounded game-design values rather than false historical precision.
7. Magic has explicit physical effects, vessels, limits, failure, maintenance, scarcity, and ordinary fallback.
8. Important omissions are documented as intentional noncraftability rather than left ambiguous.
9. A final cross-region audit verifies diversity, chain coherence, duplicate control, and static/runtime honesty.

## 8. Recommended Later Sequence

### Pass A: item manufacturability and acquisition classification audit

Classify important item families as craftable, refinable, gatherable, imported, institution-made, loot-only, unique, or unresolved.

No recipe or item edits.

### Pass B: production-family coverage matrix

Map existing recipes and chains against:

- food and preservation;
- textiles and leather;
- wood and construction;
- metal and equipment;
- pottery, glass, and containers;
- tools and agriculture;
- medicine and alchemy;
- maintenance and salvage;
- magical and institutional production.

Identify missing family-level coverage without inventing quantities.

### Pass C: regional production-diversity audit

Compare each macro region's resources, imports, exports, workplaces, institutions, and cultural identity against current recipes and finished goods.

### Pass D: exact bounded static expansion packages

Authorize separate coherent batches by production family or region. Each batch must have exact records, complete references, explicit quantity provenance, collision scans, semantic validation, and no runtime claims.

### Pass E: crafting runtime owner-contract decision

Only after sufficient static coverage exists, decide the narrowest executable crafting slice with command/result, inventory, time, tool/workplace, persistence, replay, correction, and accepted-only UI boundaries.

### Pass F: final production maturity audit

Verify regional diversity, item classification, chain closure, maintenance coverage, and honest separation between static canon and executable behavior.
