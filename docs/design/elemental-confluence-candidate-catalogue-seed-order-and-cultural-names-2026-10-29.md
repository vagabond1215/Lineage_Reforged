## 1. Governing product intent

The game should have a **preauthored, bounded catalogue of physically plausible elemental confluences** from which a seeded world may select a variable number of *religious orders*; the number of generated orders is controlled by **a seed-selected count between an as-yet-UNDECIDED floor and ceiling**, not by one universal fixed list or arbitrary unbounded generated organizations. Not all confluence candidates must exist in every world. A single qualifying confluence may inspire **zero, one, or several rival orders**, depending on source-valid plot rules and seed. Same element grouping may support several orders in different cultures and continents.

Confluence selection should respect accepted intrinsic elements **Fire, Water, Earth, Wind, Thunder, Ice, Light, Darkness**. Religious `Stone` is the legacy/applied label for canonical Earth, not a separate ninth element.

**IMPORTANT:** a mathematical grouping of elements is merely a *candidate grouping*, not proof that the world contains its physical confluence or that any religious institution exists there. A physical confluence must be established from owner-certified environmental/elemental/magical/site evidence at a coherent location and time, or a valid historical event with provenance. Static presence of two labels in one settlement is insufficient. The order selection follows world-generative eligibility and is not a new consequence of merely visiting a shrine.

The accepted elemental decision says `Light: Water/Wind/Ice`, `Darkness: Fire/Earth/Thunder` for metaphysical compatibility **and says alignment is not morality or guaranteed friendly behavior**. This does **not** prohibit a Fire/Water/Ice order if a real confluence qualifies. Light and Darkness manifestations remain rare and exceptional, so they cannot be ordinary high-frequency decorations just because every combination is mathematically expressible.

### Mathematical candidate-space bounds

Eight canonical elements yield eight single-element religious roots. For purely *combinatorial* sets of two or more distinct elements, there are `2^8 - 8 - 1 = 247` possible non-single sets, comprising 28 pairs, 56 triples, 70 quadruples, 56 quintuples, 28 six-element groups, eight seven-element groups, and one all-eight group. **This does not assert 247 physical confluences exist.** Any catalogue/executable system must distinguish `theoretically expressible`, `draft plausible`, `site-eligible`, `seed-selected`, and `observed/known to this character`.

Single-element traditions are allowed **without** inventing a multi-element convergence. The existing six religion-owned `religious_order.*` identities are authored examples, **not** the floor, ceiling or mandatory entire output of each new seed. Whether the six are historical constants or selectable per-world remains open.

## 4. Seeding an internally coherent variable number of orders

Recommended prospective generation phases, **not implemented**:

1. World creation receives a world/campaign identity, validated seed, selected world era/biome/cultural sources and creation-locked gameplay options. Base element canon is immutable.
2. Read a **curated candidate confluence bank** and owner-verified source evidence; select a feasible subset subject to rarity, geography, existing places, era, unique/deduplicated identities and special Light/Darkness gates.
3. Determine the **number of institutions** using future player-selected **minimum and maximum**. These are intentionally not assigned numerical values now; a future owner must specify how to handle zero-eligible candidates, an impossible floor, duplicate-order allowances and population limits. The number of orders is **not equal** to the number of sites or distinct combinations.
4. Allocate orders among valid single-element roots and supported multi-element confluences. Permit multiple competing/variant orders for the same combination and no orders for some otherwise valid confluences.
5. Choose an appropriate **regional/cultural name bank**, language/translation aliases, public title and unique institutional identity. Name similarity must not collapse separate orders; different public names do not necessarily prove different underlying identities.
6. Assign founding age, public/historical narrative, legitimacy, organizational structure, present status, actual power, financial resources, cultural reach, public reputation, hidden status and known political relationships in ways mutually consistent with the seed.
7. Generate inter-order alliances, rivalry, annexation, competition for followers, dependence and secret influence **as supported story premises with source-certified causality**, not as contradictions between independent random attributes.
8. Commit the generated world's history **before first character creation** and retain it across later characters, heirs and selected world chronology. Only accepted post-start world events may change it.
9. Publish only allowed public and character-observed views. The full seeded truth of hidden orders, covert identities or disputed confluences is not a visible character-creation spoiler.
10. Verify uniqueness, references, world placement, name provenance, floor/ceiling, resource consistency, observer leakage, reproducibility policy, heir continuity and re-entry. Reject seed generation when the constraints are impossible rather than silently adding implausible confluences or arbitrary orders.

## 5. Culture/language based order-name bank, not race stereotypes

The bank should be keyed by **canonical elemental combination + culture/language/tradition + geographic history + organizational form**, with race/ancestry an influence only where authored culture establishes it. Avoid treating "goblin" or "elf" as a single language and culture across all continents. Merfolk may have distinct maritime idioms in some places, but a river or coastal multiethnic community can share institutions and names across ancestry. Humans, elves and dwarves may adopt **shared regional conventions when intermingled**, while culturally isolated branches can retain distinct languages. Orc and goblin naming may differ by actual cultural group, not only species label.

## 6. Challenges/questions for future user confirmation

- How many orders min/max should an ordinary seeded world be able to start with? Is there a per-continent cap or independent culture density?
- Should all physical confluences be natural, or can magic produce stable and temporary ones?
- Can a seed independently select **where** a confluence exists, or only from already authored eligible location profiles?
- Is a historical extinct confluence valid as an order-founding origin, even when its present physical site is gone?
- Can two institutions in the same world have identical public names if they differ by culture or region?
- Do religions beginning from single elements require a confluence (recommended no) or simply an elemental affiliation plus cultural tradition?
- How do extinct institutions, dormant confluences and rumored ones count against generation floors/ceilings?
- Are large multi-element confluences extraordinarily rare, or can they be common in high-magic regions?
- What distinction, if any, separates a religion from an order, temple/chapter, cult, sect or umbrella Pantheon institution?

## 7. Current source reality and guardrails

`world.religions` currently has the Pantheon and six nested religious orders. `world.sacred_sites` has one active named Glasswake Shrine; it has **no** specific deity, religious-order or physical-confluence dedication. The `religious_site.convergence_site` entry is a *structure category*, not proof of an instantiated confluence. `magic_service.ritual_religious` is a *service vocabulary/metadata source*, not a physically observed phenomenon or miracle engine. No dedicated accepted confluence-site collection or generator is established by these files. The candidate banks above are **planning output only**.

Future confluence authoring must satisfy the actual elemental world/magic/site owner's evidence semantics, preserve higher-element rarity, retain the religious character-vs-intrinsic-element boundary, and keep knowledge/spoiler publication owner-controlled. Do not derive confluence facts from storytelling text or a seed label alone.

No changes in this design pass to master, Codex native v3, Save, Area/Atlas PR #45, tools/stations PR #40, existing world/guild/quest/magic JSON, schemas, tests, UI or game version. All catalogue rows are design candidates, **not deployed content or executable tests**. Hold for the next Religion/World-Generation named consumer and user decisions.
