Date: 2026-09-29. Repository: `vagabond1215/Lineage_Reforged` only. Inspected clean synchronized `master` at `a16523d8e08b14816a75db95e0b135c09ae40fc9`. ## Disposition

**RETENTION_BOUNDARY_ACCEPTED; HIGHER_CAPACITY_BACKEND_REQUIRED; COMBAT_ADMISSION_HELD.** The 5,242,880 UTF-16 byte ceiling is the Slice C test model for the current `localStorage` implementation. It is not a product save-size limit. A long-running campaign must retain legitimate accepted campaign/history state. The accumulating full-size candidate envelopes are clearly transient after completed publication, subject to the proof and conflict gates below; completed recovery and new-campaign attempt copies also have their existing exact completion owners. Candidate cleanup can reduce current pressure but cannot establish long-running capacity. Select IndexedDB as the target local campaign persistence backend, with transactional account/campaign publication authority and explicit quota, migration, backup and recovery behavior. This decision does not implement either change or accept ordinary combat.

The measured Slice C run used the real creator-to-World nearby-exploration caller and production save APIs against an isolated quota-enforcing in-memory `Storage`. At the creator save plus 50 action saves, retained storage was 5,150,828 bytes, including 2,545,248 candidate bytes, 2,544,942 immutable artifact bytes, a 56,790-byte slot address and a 2,608-byte QA account profile. Action 51's recovery write proposed 5,277,474 bytes and failed; its just-written candidate remained while the prior head revision 51 stayed loadable. Removing *all* action-50 candidate bytes gives an arithmetic counterfactual of 2,605,580 bytes, not a measured post-cleanup result. The 51 artifacts average about 49,901 bytes each; 1,000 copies at that observed average would occupy about 49.9 million bytes before account, slot, receipts, indexes or growing snapshots. This is a scale illustration, not a forecast or a universal quota claim. No production source, tests or content changed after the Slice C checkpoint `bb24c483d7d683466d28dcace57fc1e6a723de0a` and before this decision source.

## Safe redundant-copy and failure-order contract

1. **Inventory before reclaim.** At account scope, parse and validate every candidate, artifact, control, slot, recovery, attempt, witness, legacy migration receipt/source and account reference. Build a reference graph by exact account/campaign/slot/artifact/generation/publication/request IDs and content. Do not let a selected slot hide a pending record for another campaign targeting the same slot. Preserve unknown or malformed records for diagnosis.
2. **Candidate eligibility.** A published candidate is eligible only when its raw envelope exactly matches a validated immutable artifact with the same IDs, the matching head/address publication has completed or a separately proven superseding state exists, no pending recovery/witness promotion/consumer or migration receipt requires the candidate, and no new-campaign attempt still relies on that publication. The first Soundings artifact and applied witness stay independently retained. Exact readback of surviving authority precedes removal; absence of the candidate and unchanged surviving authority are verified afterward. A crash before removal leaves an extra copy; a crash after removal remains recoverable from the verified artifact. Cleanup must be idempotent and may never create a new head.
3. **Pre-head quota failure.** The old verified control/head/slots remain playable. A candidate written before a failed recovery write is not an accepted publication. Report the quota failure and retain or quarantine that candidate until the account-wide reference graph proves it orphaned; a retry from regenerated caller state must not silently claim the old candidate or delete conflicting evidence. If safe reclaim is insufficient, stop publication without truncating accepted history and present a recoverable storage-full posture.
4. **Post-head failure.** Preserve the new artifact, control, exact recovery and any pending/applied witness. Complete address and account consumers through the existing owner before allowing a conflicting new publication. Validate current slot authority before projection so stale recovery cannot overwrite a newer or different publication. Fail closed on multiple recoveries for one slot, lost caller state, missing witness, incomplete consumer evidence or non-head conflict.
5. **No artifact/history GC in this package.** A later artifact compaction decision would need an explicit product contract for historical checkpoint access, forks, first-publication provenance, account history and backup equivalence. It must prove the complete reference graph and migrated read paths first. Neither age, ancestry nor the 5 MiB model is a deletion criterion.

Candidate cleanup is a bridge and must be tested under the current 5 MiB model, including temporary peak writes and the action-51 orphan. It is **not** a combat-capacity acceptance gate by itself: immutable artifacts and future accepted receipts continue to grow.

## Higher-capacity backend disposition

**Migration required for long-running local campaigns.** Current `localStorage` writes are synchronous full-string writes spread across `saveManager`, account profile, new-campaign attempts and launcher callers. The measured duplicate reduction leaves artifact growth and future campaign/account history growth. Repeating the observed sequence can exhaust the current store even if candidates are reclaimed. The product should therefore not rely on a fixed `localStorage` budget or on deletion of durable history.

Select **IndexedDB** for the local campaign/account data store: its keyed records, indexes and read-write transactions fit artifact, head, slot, witness, recovery, migration and account-consumer relationships. The [IndexedDB specification](https://www.w3.org/TR/IndexedDB/) defines transactional record operations; the [Storage Standard](https://storage.spec.whatwg.org/) provides origin quota estimates and persistence requests. This is an architecture choice, not a claim of unlimited or device-independent storage. Browser quota varies; a transaction can fail, an origin can be cleared, and best-effort data may be evicted. Use `navigator.storage.estimate()` only as an estimate, request/inspect persistent storage where supported, and provide a user-controlled export/restore path before relying on local storage as the only long-term copy. The [browser quota and eviction guidance](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Storage_API/Storage_quotas_and_eviction_criteria) makes those limits explicit.

Keep pure game engines unaware of browser storage. Introduce one asynchronous persistence owner behind typed operations, then adapt launcher/session callers. A publication must validate source and current head, write immutable artifact plus head/slot and required witness/account-consumer facts with a transaction or a staged recovery whose commit point and exact readback are explicit. Do not await unrelated work inside an open IndexedDB transaction. Keep stable campaign, artifact, publication, request and receipt IDs and existing semantic validators. Current `saves.v7`/`accounts.v1`/attempt keys remain a read-only legacy source during migration, not a second writable truth. Copy and validate into the new store, atomically mark the new authority active, then re-read after restart before allowing writes. If migration aborts, old data remain loadable; if both stores diverge, quarantine and require explicit resolution rather than choosing whichever is newer. Cross-tab/stale-client conflicts and account reset must be part of the cutover test. Retire old campaign keys only in a separately verified cleanup after rollback/export requirements are met; preserve launcher-owned keys.

Compared alternatives: continued `localStorage` with candidate cleanup is a temporary bridge and still has a small fixed write ceiling; OPFS is useful for file-shaped large data but would require a separate multi-record commit protocol for these keyed authorities and remains quota-managed; remote/cloud persistence would add account, privacy and deployment ownership not authorized here. IndexedDB needs no new production dependency, but requires async caller changes and browser integration tests. No actual browser-support matrix, persistence-grant rate or available quota has been measured for this app, so no fixed capacity guarantee is claimed.

## Acceptance gates and staged implementation

The next package is **DEV-0.7.1 Slice D - Transactional Campaign Store Foundation**, an internal prerequisite of the held parent. It owns only a versioned IndexedDB store contract and one bounded transaction/readback implementation with isolated tests; no live cutover, user-save mutation, artifact deletion or combat. The first checkpoint is a committed, tested transaction boundary for artifact/control/slot/witness records, with abort/quota and stale-head behavior. Later separate slices own localStorage-to-IndexedDB copy/verify/cutover, async account/launcher caller integration, candidate lifecycle cleanup and legacy retirement. Do not place a new production dependency in any slice without separate approval. The parent remains held until all required storage and ordinary encounter acceptance gates pass.

| Finding | Owner and smallest repair | Required proof before acceptance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| F1: completed candidate copies dominate avoidable growth | Save-publication owner, after exact reference inventory; bounded candidate cleanup bridge | Ordinary creator/action/save plus orphan quota, restart, same-source and regenerated retry, Soundings first witness, migration pending/applied, non-head and cross-slot cases; unchanged artifacts/head/witness/account bytes. |
| F2: immutable history still exceeds current fixed store over time | IndexedDB store owner, then async campaign/account caller migration | Transaction abort/quota leaves old accepted state; 1,000 action/save cycles in one isolated campaign plus a second campaign, non-head slot and accepted Soundings witness; reload/restart, account consumers, migration and exact readback. This is an engineering stress scenario, not a product save-count cap. |
| F3: full slot envelope and pending recovery duplicate payloads | Versioned address and transactional publication owners, after backend authority exists | Compact pointer migration only after old and new loaders agree; interrupted publication, competing/stale recovery, deleted slot, pending consumer and first witness remain correct. |
| F4: local browser storage is not a permanent backup | Persistence/launcher product owner | Dynamic quota and denied persistence are visible; storage-full leaves prior save usable; export/restore round trip and legacy rollback are tested before old copies are retired. |

Measure retained and attempted peak bytes/records across *all* current localStorage keys during the bridge. For IndexedDB acceptance, report actual browser usage estimates alongside exact serialized campaign/account bytes and the successful 1,000-save workload; do not impose a universal 5 MiB, 50-save or 1,000-save product limit. Inject quota and abort at each publication phase, then verify exact accepted head, slot, witness and account state after restart. Test at least two supported browsers before declaring backend capacity sufficient; if a required browser lacks usable IndexedDB or storage is unavailable, fail closed with read/export/recovery guidance. Future repeated combat admission/outcome/defeat receipts need their own measured workload after implementation; Slice C's exploration sequence cannot accept them.
