## 1. Governing principle

**Elemental magic and manifestations are real; the gods' existence is genuinely uncertain. Religion is a variety of human and cultural responses to elemental life and power, not supernatural proof.**

People can understand that elements manifest as elementals and conclude from this that elements themselves live, that magic gives rise to life, or that the elemental powers govern life, death, light, darkness, heat, gold, and the material world. These are **in-world interpretations**, not objective metaphysical facts automatically established by religion content. Magic is a tool, sometimes respected, sometimes abhorred, and sometimes both. Characters may view elementals as sacred persons, living natural powers, exploitable magical resources, frightening predators, or things demanding investigation.

Faith may arise from tradition, devotion, fascination, emotion, social conformity, fear, personal resonance, family obligations, respect, ideology, pragmatic alliance, concealed motives, opportunism or rejection. Devout, indifferent, tolerant, performative, pluralistic and openly anti-religious characters are all legitimate possibilities. Religion may be socially unavoidable in many places without being mechanically mandatory for every character.

**No miracle system is presently intended.** Prayer, ritual and offerings do not automatically heal, create spells, restore life, confer blessings or prove divine agency. Fictional zealots may mistakenly believe surrendering themselves to an elemental grants rebirth. They may instead suffer ordinary harm and death. Any injury, death, response or other consequence must be decided by its owning mechanics and authored story, not generated by religious prose.

## 3. Historical shape and institutional personality

The Pantheon may be a culturally shared framework encompassing numerous ancient single-element traditions, devotional churches and other forms. The oldest powerful religious lineages should begin from individual elements. Over centuries, some may spread across continents; some may develop into pluralistic orders; others may lose temples, disappear from records, become secret movements, be absorbed by rivals, or reinvent themselves behind apparently secular institutions.

**Orders should have coherent personalities grounded in their component elements**, expressed as social virtues, taboos, arts, service roles, institutional temperament and theological interpretations. This is cultural characterization, not a new magic-affinity algorithm. A pair or group of elements should make religious sense through compatibility, complementarity, historical reconciliation or locally argued confluence. Mixed orders need not erase single-element ancestry or suppress their internal debates.

The existing Pantheon has six named nested organizations: Luminous Tide (Light/Water), Gale Veil (Wind/Ice), Forge Sanctum (Fire/Stone/Earth), Stormbound Covenant (Thunder/Darkness), Prismatic Enclave (all eight), and the Unbound (Darkness/Fire/Thunder). These remain **authored existing religious-order descriptions**, not implemented membership and governance systems. The accepted elemental affinity decision places Light with Water, Wind, Ice and Darkness with Fire, Earth, Thunder; alignment is metaphysical rather than moral. Do not treat these six examples as the only possible orders or rename their canonical IDs in this pass.

**Potential story examples, not canon:**
- A Darkness religion persecuted centuries ago may hide inside a practical institution, perhaps an adventuring network, to rebuild its power or patiently influence a continent. **The existing \`guild.adventurers_guild\` is not revealed or changed by this idea.**
- A conquered or annexed single-element temple could leave an asymmetrical gap in the present religious landscape. Descendants or surviving devotees might seek peaceful restoration, reform, political leverage or revenge from inside the conquering order.
- Fringe cults could venerate living elemental manifestations, misinterpret mortal death as an opportunity for rebirth, and cause tragic or destructive events without earning divine resurrection.
- A widespread accepted Pantheon need not entail one centralized priesthood or unanimous truth.

Religion-specific histories, masked affiliations, institutional identities, clergy and secret influence must be properly authored and discovered in play. No current source record licenses fabricated historical dates, covert NPCs, new faction identifiers, riots, enforcement, service access or conspiracy revelations.

## 4. Magic, healing, actual death and Echo boundaries

- Multiple/all elements may support actual healing **by distinct owner-approved methods**; lore descriptions of Lady of Light healing do not prove that only Light heals. A Water practitioner might eventually restore through one mechanism, Earth through another, etc., but examples are **not spells added here**.
- A possible Light-specific **true resurrection** must be decided later with the magic, health, mortality and Stakes owners. Do not treat resurrection, ordinary treatment, HP restoration, "recovery_pending", final closure or reincarnation as synonyms.
- Existing accepted mortality design distinguishes actual death, restoration-eligible actual death, final death and mode-specific permanent closure. Ironbound terminality and other protected Stakes restrictions remain controlling.
- Echo-style reincarnation could be a high-tier bounded inheritance or continuity trait for an **already legitimate heir**, preserving memories/aptitudes or legacy traces from a fully exhausted predecessor. Whether the original soul actually returns can remain an in-world mystery. This is distinct from reversing actual death or reopening the previous character.
- Existing heir plans require real offspring or adoption evidence and proper Prestige heir capacity. A new Echo option must not mint people, manufacture a family relationship, duplicate possessions, grant historical religious ranks, or bypass existing creator, Save and death owners.
- Speculation about religious reincarnation is optional: a faith may believe it occurred without being required for the feature to function.

## 6. Critical unresolved questions for next user round

1. Is the shared Pantheon an umbrella over independent single-element religions, a historical union, or both?
2. Are the eight named "gods" separate personalities asserted by ordinary people, literal elemental consciousnesses, or culturally invented faces of forces?
3. Do religious people consider elementals fundamentally divine, sacred animals, living magic, or dangerous independent beings?
4. How much can traditions vary in local names, rituals and teachings before they are considered separate religions?
5. Do the oldest single-element faiths still hold dominant institutions, or have mixed orders displaced many of them?
6. Are mixed orders primarily founded through compatible elements, political unions or deliberate oppositional reconciliations?
7. Is the Darkness tradition generally persecuted or only suppressed by particular governments/orders/cultures?
8. Does any religious order control the Adventurers Guild in future canon, or should the hidden-order premise remain merely one possible storyline?
9. What does participation look like for a skeptic, a believer, a fearful pragmatist and a secret apostate?
10. Is religion-specific standing mostly public reputation, member standing or privately held belief?
11. Are sect sacrifices usually voluntary self-harm, coerced harms, false rumors or rare dramatic criminal storylines? No actual mechanic assumed.
12. How important is religious control of places, schools, charity, trade, courts, armies and land over long history?
13. Should magic colleges and religious institutions cooperate, compete or vary widely by culture?
14. Could true resurrection ever be only Light, or should no element have an absolute monopoly?
15. If an exhausted character's Echo shapes an heir, is that literal soul reincarnation, a bounded inheritance imprint, or deliberately unknowable?
16. Should religion be *entirely optional* for Echo/Lineage and succession even if religious characters interpret it theologically?
17. What is the first safe content focus: historical single-element churches, six existing mixed orders, rituals and cultural practices, or secrets and religious schisms?
18. Which specific events, if any, should become approved initial historical canon rather than speculative possibilities?
