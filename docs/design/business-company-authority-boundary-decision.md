# Business Company Authority Boundary Decision

Source version/run: Version 0.5.335 - Business Company Authority Boundary Decision

Status: approved documentation-only owner boundary; no schema/content permission

## 1. Decision Summary

Approve one future narrow static commercial-body identity authority in principle:

- conceptual authority: `civilization.businesses`;
- conceptual canonical identity prefix: `business.`;
- `business` is the broad identity family;
- `company` is a possible descriptive business form, not a separate first-pass identity owner.

The authority may eventually identify durable named commercial bodies that persist independently of current owners, workers, facilities, property, inventory, contracts, or ledgers. It must not own operating state or collapse economy, provider, property, account, quest, or runtime systems.

No current `business.*` or `company.*` string is promoted by this decision. Carry forward exactly zero candidate ids. Select `Version 0.5.336 - Business Authority Schema Plan` next as a documentation-only, content-free contract plan.

- Generated `company.<settlement>.<districtType>` ids remain synthetic property owner/operator placeholders.

## 3. Business Versus Company

Use `business` as the broad future static identity family because:

- current authored quest, account, UI, and demo surfaces already use business terminology;
- it can describe an enduring commercial body without requiring a modern or formal company concept;
- `company` can be represented later as a descriptive form when canon supports it;
- a separate company collection would create avoidable overlap with partnerships, cooperatives, sole-trader ventures, merchant houses, and other commercial bodies.

This is a future authority decision, not a declaration that every current business string is canonical.

`company` remains:

- a possible later classification/form value for a canonical business;
- an existing synthetic owner/operator label in settlement derivation;
- ordinary display-language in **Ironwheel Haulage Company**.

Those meanings do not create a second static owner. Do not rename or migrate generated `company.*` ids in this phase.

## 4. Future Static Identity Boundary

A future first-pass business record may own only stable descriptive identity:

- canonical id and slug;
- canonical display name;
- lifecycle/status;
- narrow commercial-body category or form only when the schema plan proves a safe vocabulary;
- descriptive public posture only when non-behavioral;
- static summary;
- source authority/provenance notes;
- non-implication notes.

The identity should answer: **Which enduring named commercial body is this?**

It must not answer:

- who owns, manages, works for, supplies, sponsors, licenses, or represents it;
- where it operates or what property it controls;
- what it currently produces, sells, buys, stores, ships, owes, earns, or spends;
- whether the player can access, inherit, manage, trade with, trust, or receive quests from it;
- what runtime state, UI, save data, or gameplay behavior it has.

## 5. Separate Existing And Future Owners

### Templates and places

- Buildings retain facility-template identity and `triggerBusinessTypes` compatibility vocabulary.
- Workplaces retain production/workforce templates, `businessScale`, jobs, tools, inputs, outputs, and upgrades.
- Settlements, districts, sites, plots, facilities, and placed property remain place/property anchors.

### Services and providers

- `civilization.services` retains provider-independent service vocabulary.
- Provider associations, availability, service area, access, payment, and execution require later link/runtime decisions.
- A business record's existence does not make it a provider.

### People and organizations

- People/NPCs retain personal identity and presence.
- Guilds, institutions, factions, polities, governments, religions/orders, families/households, and professions/roles retain their identities.
- Owners, founders, shareholders, partners, managers, contacts, employees, members, and representatives require later typed links or mutable state.

### Property, estate, and account

- Property ownership, leases, holdings, branches, facilities, and operating sites remain separate.
- Account estate retains deposits, claims, inheritance, asset ownership/operating state, and stored summaries.
- A future static business id may be referenced only after a separate exact link decision; current `business.*` account ids are not automatically catalog references.

### Economy and runtime

- Settlement simulation retains derived businesses, synthetic owners/operators, workforce, input/output goods, infrastructure dependencies, and current state.
- Economy/runtime owners retain stock, inventory, storage contents, prices, sales, purchases, contracts, shipments, routes, revenue, expenses, profit, payroll, treasury, debt, valuation, taxes, upgrades, and ledgers.
- Runtime/UI/save systems retain current activity, health/operating state, player management, and projections.

### Narrative and social state

- Quest definitions retain giver presentation, contacts, requirements, schedules, branches, outcomes, and reward behavior.
- Reputation, trust, standing, favorability, access, legal status, recognition, dialogue, events, and relationships remain separate mutable or narrative owners.

## 6. Commercial Form And Edge-Case Posture

### Companies and partnerships

An explicitly authored durable company or partnership may later qualify as a business identity. The first schema plan may consider a narrow descriptive form/category vocabulary, but this decision does not approve values or records.

### Sole traders

Defer. A sole trader can conflate a person, trade name, provider role, and business asset. It requires canonical person identity plus a person-to-business ownership/operator boundary.

### Branches

Defer. A branch is normally a placed operating unit or relationship between a business and a site/settlement, not a second business by default. Branch identity, parent links, locations, and operating state need a later decision.

### Brands and trade names

Defer. A brand may be a display mark, alias, product family, or intangible asset rather than an organized commercial body. The first contract should not include brand records or alias behavior.

### Ventures and projects

Defer. Temporary ventures, expeditions, contracts, operations, and projects are activity/runtime concepts unless explicit canon establishes an enduring body.

### Merchant houses

Defer. A merchant house may be a family/lineage, guild-like body, noble house, business, or combination. It requires a separate boundary or explicit authored classification.

### Generated operators

Exclude. Synthetic `company.*`, household, guild, civic-authority, temple, noble, or player owner/operator ids remain derived projections and cannot seed static business records.
