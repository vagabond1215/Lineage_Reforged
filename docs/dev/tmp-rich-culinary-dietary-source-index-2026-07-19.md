# Rich Culinary And Dietary Research Source Index

Access date for web sources: 2026-07-20. External evidence is advisory: it supports factual framing and candidate design constraints, but it does not create Lineage: Reforged canon, balance numbers, edibility rules, recipes, or runtime authority.

## External Sources

### S01 — FoodData Central help and data documentation

- Organization: U.S. Department of Agriculture, Agricultural Research Service
- Publication/update date: continuously updated; Foundation Foods download listed April 2026
- URL: https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/help/ and https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/download-datasets/
- Source type: official nutrient database documentation
- Topic: food-component values, serving context, analytical metadata, and nutrient-data provenance
- Supported claims: nutrient values are food- and sample-specific data with supporting metadata; a game should use a declared abstraction and portion basis rather than imply that generic item names are measured foods.
- Limitations: modern human-food data; not direct authority for fictional species, fantasy lineages, recipe yields, game balance, or historical portions.
- Narrative use: Domain 4; candidate nutrition models; rejected false precision.

### S02 — Minimum Dietary Diversity for Women (MDD-W)

- Organization: Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations and partners
- Publication/update date: guide revision 2021; web page accessed 2026-07-20
- URL: https://www.fao.org/nutrition/assessment/tools/minimum-dietary-diversity-women
- Source type: official dietary-assessment guidance
- Topic: dietary diversity and food-group coverage
- Supported claims: diet quality includes adequacy, moderation, balance, and diversity; no single food supplies every needed nutrient; food-group variety can be a coarse proxy for micronutrient adequacy.
- Limitations: the five-of-ten threshold is validated for women aged 15–49 at population level, not as a universal individual requirement or a game mechanic. This report uses only the diversity principle, not the threshold.
- Narrative use: Domains 4, 5, and 8; moderate model and hearty-pack composition.

### S03 — Dietary reference values

- Organization: European Food Safety Authority
- Publication/update date: DRV scientific-opinion program completed 2019; topic page updated 2024
- URL: https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/dietary-reference-values
- Source type: official scientific guidance overview
- Topic: energy, water, protein, fat, carbohydrate, fibre, vitamin, and mineral reference values
- Supported claims: nutrition has multiple dimensions and reference values are population/scientific-policy tools; the present energy/protein/hydration runtime represents only a subset.
- Limitations: EFSA explicitly distinguishes scientific advice from individual prescriptions; values are modern and must not be copied into fantasy balance without a separate abstraction decision.
- Narrative use: Domain 4; limits of the live profile; rejected micronutrient simulation.

### S04 — Effect of dairy proteins on appetite: review of controlled clinical trials

- Organization/authors: Bendtsen et al.; peer-reviewed review, *Advances in Nutrition*
- Publication/update date: 2013/2014
- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3941822/
- Source type: peer-reviewed review
- Topic: protein and satiety
- Supported claims: protein is generally more satiating than fat or carbohydrate in the reviewed evidence, while source-specific effects and study results vary.
- Limitations: human modern-diet evidence, much of it dairy-specific; does not justify a universal exact protein-to-fullness formula.
- Narrative use: Domain 4; moderate and rich model weighting.

### S05 — The effect of fiber on satiety and food intake: a systematic review

- Organization/authors: Clark and Slavin; peer-reviewed systematic review
- Publication/update date: 2013
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23885994/
- Source type: peer-reviewed systematic review
- Topic: fibre, subjective appetite, and subsequent intake
- Supported claims: fibre effects are heterogeneous; most acute treatments in the review did not improve satiety or reduce intake, although some fibre types had repeated support.
- Limitations: acute human studies with varied fibres and methods; supports caution and a coarse/optional fibre signal, not a deterministic bonus.
- Narrative use: Domain 4; uncertainty notes; rejection of a strong universal fibre multiplier.

### S06 — Dietary fat, fibre, satiation, and satiety

- Organization/authors: Warrilow et al.; peer-reviewed systematic review and meta-analysis
- Publication/update date: 2018
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30166637/
- Source type: peer-reviewed systematic review
- Topic: energy density, fat, fibre, satiation, and satiety
- Supported claims: fat is energy-dense and had weak satiation per unit energy in this review; the combined fat/fibre meta-analysis did not establish a significant satiety effect.
- Limitations: only twelve studies met inclusion criteria; the authors identify design sensitivity limits. It does not support penalizing fat as nutritionally bad.
- Narrative use: Domain 4; separation of energy density from fullness.

### S07 — Volume of food consumed affects satiety in men

- Organization/authors: Rolls et al.; *American Journal of Clinical Nutrition*
- Publication/update date: 1998
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9625090/
- Source type: peer-reviewed controlled clinical trial
- Topic: volume and satiety at similar energy/macronutrient composition
- Supported claims: food volume can influence satiating potency independently of energy content.
- Limitations: one controlled trial in men using milk drinks; volume is one contributor and should not become a precise universal equation.
- Narrative use: Domain 4; portion/volume axis in the moderate model.

### S08 — Sensory and physical characteristics of foods that impact intake

- Organization/authors: Appleton et al.; peer-reviewed systematic review and meta-analyses
- Publication/update date: 2021
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33754456/
- Source type: peer-reviewed systematic review and meta-analysis
- Topic: texture, physical form, viscosity, volume, satiation, and subsequent intake
- Supported claims: harder, chunkier, more viscous, voluminous, or solid foods tended to produce greater satiation in the included within-subject comparisons.
- Limitations: heterogeneous products and short-term outcomes; supports a small coarse texture/form axis, not culinary realism or exact duration values.
- Narrative use: Domain 4; rich model candidate and rejection of liquid/solid equivalence.

### S09 — Assessment of satiety depends on energy density and portion size

- Organization/authors: Williams, Roe, and Rolls; peer-reviewed crossover study
- Publication/update date: 2013/2014
- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3874079/
- Source type: peer-reviewed controlled clinical trial
- Topic: energy density, portion size, and meal intake
- Supported claims: energy density and portion size can independently influence meal intake; a satiety-enhancing preload does not erase the context of the following meal.
- Limitations: forty-six women and specific salad/pasta meals; evidence for multiple axes, not a universal numerical rule.
- Narrative use: Domain 4; recommendation to keep calories and fullness separate.

### S10 — Five Keys to Safer Food manual

- Organization: World Health Organization
- Publication/update date: 15 May 2006
- URL: https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241594639
- Source type: official global food-safety manual
- Topic: hygiene, raw/cooked separation, thorough cooking, safe temperatures, and safe water/raw materials
- Supported claims: food safety is multi-factor; raw/cooked separation and clean handling matter independently of preservation labels.
- Limitations: modern public-health guidance; temperatures and time limits should remain research evidence unless a future gameplay abstraction explicitly adopts them.
- Narrative use: Domains 1, 2, and 9; safety state vocabulary and stop conditions.

### S11 — Are You Storing Food Safely?

- Organization: U.S. Food and Drug Administration
- Publication/update date: page date not relied upon; accessed 2026-07-20
- URL: https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/are-you-storing-food-safely
- Source type: official consumer food-safety guidance
- Topic: perishable-food refrigeration, time/temperature exposure, storage, and cross-contamination
- Supported claims: cold storage slows microbial growth but does not make food indefinitely safe; perishables and ready-to-eat foods need different handling context.
- Limitations: modern refrigerator/freezer guidance; unsuitable as direct medieval-fantasy mechanics or exact shelf-life tables.
- Narrative use: Domain 9; recommendation for observable storage bands rather than copied real-world timers.

### S12 — Code of Practice for Fish and Fishery Products, second edition

- Organization: Codex Alimentarius Commission / FAO and WHO
- Publication/update date: 2012 edition
- URL: https://www.fao.org/4/i2382e/i2382e.pdf
- Source type: official technical code of practice
- Topic: fish preparation, salting, drying, hot/cold smoking, hazards, defects, packaging, and storage
- Supported claims: salting, drying, hot smoking, cold smoking, and smoke-drying are distinct processes; preservation outcome depends on controlled moisture, salt, heat, hygiene, packaging, and storage.
- Limitations: technical modern code with process measurements; this report uses only distinctions and hazards, not production specifications.
- Narrative use: Domains 2, 3, and 9; preserved-state vocabulary and recipe prerequisites.

### S13 — Life in a castle

- Organization: English Heritage
- Publication/update date: page date not stated; accessed 2026-07-20
- URL: https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/castles/life-in-a-castle/
- Source type: museum/heritage historical synthesis
- Topic: medieval household kitchens, bread grades, fish days, salted winter foods, spices, labor, and social status
- Supported claims: historical foodways linked preparation, season, wealth, institution, labor, and display; food names and menu identity need not be nutrient labels.
- Limitations: English castle context, not universal medieval practice and not Lineage canon.
- Narrative use: Domains 6, 7, and 10; naming, hospitality, feast, and class distinctions.

### S14 — Early medieval England food and diet explored in new bioarchaeological studies

- Organization: University of Cambridge Department of Archaeology; summary of work by Leggett and Lambert
- Publication/update date: 22 April 2022
- URL: https://www.arch.cam.ac.uk/news/early-medieval-england-food-and-diet-explored-new-bioarchaeological-studies
- Source type: university research summary linked to peer-reviewed studies
- Topic: cereal-based ordinary diets, pottage, small amounts of animal foods, and occasional feasts
- Supported claims: exceptional feast lists should not be treated as everyday menus; ordinary diet and elite display can require different content structures.
- Limitations: early medieval England and a research-news summary; useful as analogy only.
- Narrative use: Domains 6–8; everyday, hearty, and luxury pack distinctions.

### S15 — “To eat or not to eat”: dairy and meat alternatives

- Organization: Fitzwilliam Museum, University of Cambridge
- Publication/update date: page date not stated; accessed 2026-07-20
- URL: https://feast-and-fast.fitzmuseum.cam.ac.uk/discover/to-eat-or-not
- Source type: museum historical synthesis
- Topic: religious fasting, fish days, pulses, nuts, vegetables, seafood, and dairy alternatives
- Supported claims: dietary restriction can reshape ingredient networks, menus, institutions, and naming rather than act as a single character debuff.
- Limitations: European 1500–1800 context extends beyond the medieval period; no Lineage religion or observance rule is inferred.
- Narrative use: Domains 5–7; restriction-authority questions.

### S16 — Plants of the World Online / World Checklist of Vascular Plants methodology

- Organization: Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew
- Publication/update date: WCVP/POWO updated continuously; accessed 2026-07-20
- URL: https://powo.science.kew.org/about-wcvp
- Source type: expert-reviewed taxonomic database methodology
- Topic: accepted plant names, synonyms, distributions, references, and taxonomic uncertainty
- Supported claims: real-plant identity checks should separate accepted name, synonymy, distribution, and reported uses; a common name alone cannot establish exact species or edibility.
- Limitations: no bulk species-by-species validation was performed in this run; fictional flora require project authorship, and a reported “food” use does not prove preparation safety.
- Narrative use: Domain 1; source-verification method and stop conditions.

### S17 — FishBase manual

- Organization: FishBase Consortium
- Publication/update date: living database/manual; accessed 2026-07-20
- URL: https://www.fishbase.se/manual/english/
- Source type: scientific species database documentation
- Topic: fish taxonomy, ecology, common names, uses, diet, and processing information
- Supported claims: fish common names can map to multiple biological records and should be verified against taxonomy/ecology before source-specific edibility or regional claims.
- Limitations: no bulk species-by-species validation was performed; repository fish records are setting authority and may be deliberately generalized.
- Narrative use: Domains 1 and 6; future source qualification.

## Evidence Use Rule

Repository facts take precedence for current behavior and canon. External sources can justify distinctions, caution, terminology, or candidate abstractions. They cannot establish a fictional species as edible, assign a nutrient number, authorize a recipe quantity, create a regional custom, or enable runtime behavior. Those remain `authored_input_required`, `bounded_design_inference`, or `unresolved` as marked in the matrix.
