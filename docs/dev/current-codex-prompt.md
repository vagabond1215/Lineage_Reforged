## Required Research Questions

### 1. Environmental affinity pressure

Research transferable models for:

- environmental fields, gradients, source/sink behavior, thresholds, hysteresis, and locality;
- how climate, terrain, water, geology, vegetation, settlement activity, magic use, and authored sites might contribute evidence without becoming one generic scalar owner;
- ambient manifestation versus discrete entity manifestation;
- when an entity persists independently from the field that produced or attracted it;
- reinforcement, depletion, recovery, migration, dormancy, and decay.

Do not select exact elements, weights, equations, thresholds, or biome mappings.

### 2. Entity identity and taxonomy

Research ontology patterns that keep separate:

- substance or embodiment;
- origin;
- agency;
- sapience;
- persistence;
- created versus emergent status;
- binding or service role;
- guardian, territorial, cooperative, predatory, or other current function;
- biological animal, magical animal, construct, elemental, spirit, fae-like being, and institution-bound guardian.

Determine which distinctions should be stable identity, capabilities, roles, relationships, or mutable disposition. Do not import an external mythological taxonomy as canon.

### 3. Baseline temperament and current disposition

Research how to distinguish:

- stable temperament or behavioral priors;
- current disposition toward a specific actor, group, place, object, activity, or stimulus;
- physiological or magical state;
- learned familiarity, habituation, sensitization, trust, fear, territorial pressure, and resource need;
- immediate selected action.

Evaluate passive, curious, territorial, cooperative, helpful, assimilative, defensive, predatory, and guardian behavior as possible descriptive postures, not fixed species moral alignments.

### 4. Active and passive magical stimuli

Research a stimulus taxonomy that can distinguish:

- active casting, channeling, ritual, attack, healing, warding, summoning, dispelling, and deliberate manipulation;
- passive enchantments, carried artifacts, wards, residue, ambient fields, attunement, lineage traits, and latent capability;
- stimulus source, target, intensity category, duration, novelty, familiarity, compatibility, ownership, consent, and visibility;
- perceived stimulus versus hidden authoritative stimulus.

Determine which responses require perception/recognition and which may follow direct magical interaction.

### 5. Response architecture

Compare:

- closed rule/state-machine behavior;
- behavior trees;
- goal-oriented action planning;
- utility-based selection;
- belief/desire/intention or comparable agent models;
- hybrid architectures.

Evaluate determinism, explainability, authoring cost, data requirements, testing, persistence, debuggability, and fit with a foundation-stage TypeScript simulation.

Recommend the smallest safe conceptual posture for a later decision without selecting a production library or runtime package.

### 6. Deterministic response identity

Research how a future system can retain:

- normalized stimulus and context identity;
- entity state and behavioral-policy version;
- candidate options and eligibility;
- selected response and tie-break posture;
- causal evidence;
- random or seeded choice where later authorized;
- replay, save/load, and correction identity.

Hashing or canonical serialization cannot define semantic normalization by itself.

### 7. Beneficial capability authorization

Research how helpful, cooperative, protective, diagnostic, healing, transport, warning, resource-providing, or environmental capabilities remain:

- explicitly possessed;
- perceptibly motivated or triggered;
- bounded by cost, access, range, knowledge, consent, relationship, and current state;
- causally traceable;
- unable to appear as deus ex machina;
- unable to bypass treatment, magic, route, quest, economy, or consequence owners.

### 8. Warning and escalation

Research readable staged behavior:

- passive presence;
- attention or curiosity;
- warning or boundary display;
- guarded/defensive posture;
- pursuit, interception, attack, aid, withdrawal, dormancy, or other accepted action;
- de-escalation, habituation, and renewed escalation.

Separate observer-visible cues from hidden internal state. Avoid a universal hostility meter.

### 9. Persistence, reinforcement, decay, and despawning

Research:

- persistence after the originating stimulus ends;
- memory and relationship persistence;
- reinforcement and habituation;
- displacement, migration, dormancy, dispersal, dissolution, and death;
- source correction and replay;
- conservation constraints and anti-farming posture;
- when despawning is a presentation/runtime optimization versus an in-world outcome.

Do not choose exact timers or formulas.

- observer projection filters hidden identity, motive, capability, and future outcome;
