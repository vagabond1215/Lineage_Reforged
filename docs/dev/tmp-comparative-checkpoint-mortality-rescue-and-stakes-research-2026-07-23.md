## 2. Research Necessity

The proposal would materially revise accepted save topology and may revise the boundary between actual death and final closure. Repository inspection cannot decide whether checkpoint scarcity, committed randomness, derived critical states, contextual rescue, resurrection eligibility, and lineage settlement are understandable in play. Comparative evidence is therefore useful before any authority-revision pass.

This research tests patterns; it does not treat another game's popularity, terminology, or formula as authority.

## 6. Save, Checkpoint, And Anti-Reroll Findings

KCD permits manual saves through a consumable and through beds specifically marked for sleep-and-save; an exit save has separate limits. This makes saving contextual and costly but leaves a loadable historical point. Scarcity therefore limits attempts without committing an event outcome. ([S1](https://kingdomcomedeliverance.wiki.gg/wiki/Saviour_Schnapps), [S2](https://kingdomcomedeliverance.wiki.gg/wiki/Tiredness_and_Waiting))

XCOM's Ironman mode autosaves accepted actions and removes ordinary rollback. Outside Ironman, its synchronous combat random sequence produces the same results when the same actions repeat in the same order. Jake Solomon described that as preventing reload-after-miss behavior while acknowledging ways around it. ([S3](https://techland.time.com/?p=148165))

The directly transferable principle is two-layer integrity:

1. **load topology** determines how far the player may rewind;
2. **event commitment** determines whether replaying materially identical causes rerolls an outcome.

Sleep checkpoints alone are insufficient for extreme-RNG fishing. A future Lineage decision should consider stable event/draw identity. A single global random sequence is only an adaptable pattern: unrelated action ordering can become a seed-shaping exploit. This last point is an inference from XCOM's “same actions in the same order” behavior, not a sourced claim about its internal implementation.

## 7. Mortal-State And Stabilization Findings

RimWorld treats “downed” as alive but unable to act, derived from consciousness, pain, or movement capability. It separately allows on-site tending, carrying, rescue, capture, and continued attack. ([S4](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Downed))

Kenshi separates functional thresholds from wound progression: vital-part damage can cause knockout or recovery coma; untreated cuts do not simply heal; bleeding can kill; conscious actors can self-aid, while unconscious actors require another agent; bed rest accelerates recovery. ([S6](https://store.steampowered.com/app/233860/Kenshi/), [S7](https://kenshi.fandom.com/wiki/Guide_to_Health))

These comparisons argue against one universal bleed-out timer and against a redundant mutable `critical_stable` owner. The clearer model is:

```text
functional state: active | downed/unconscious
lethal processes: zero or more independently owned processes
care requirement: none | basic stabilization | definitive/professional care
presentation: stable aid-required or unstable, derived from those facts
```

An explicit player-facing stable state is meaningful for triage and explanation. It need not be separately persisted if it is deterministically derived. Stabilization should stop or bound the lethal process it addresses; it need not restore consciousness, mobility, or full recovery.

## 8. Continued Threat, Rescue, And Body Recovery

RimWorld makes rescue concrete: a downed actor can be tended in place, carried to a bed, captured, attacked, or left to crawl; most enemies disengage, but the rule is not universal. ([S4](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Downed))

Kenshi connects survival to able squad members, first-aid supplies, body-part injuries, carrying, safe beds, and enemy/ecology behavior; its official description specifically notes that blood attracts predators and squads carry wounded to safety. ([S6](https://store.steampowered.com/app/233860/Kenshi/))

Useful rescue inputs are factual and owner-specific:

- attacker goal/disposition and whether threat remains;
- conscious helpers, loyalty/permission, aid skill, supplies, carrying capacity, mounts;
- traversable escape path, terrain, weather, exposure, and travel time;
- safe destination, patrol, witness, settlement, or institution availability;
- patient/body state and transportability.

The resolver should use deterministic rules and named uncertain events, not collapse these facts into one opaque “rescue chance.” Forecasts should explain blockers and contributors.

## 9. Resurrection And Final Closure

RimWorld's resurrector serum is a rare one-use resource obtained through bounded exceptional routes rather than routine crafting or trade. It requires a non-desiccated organic corpse; elapsed unfrozen time affects adverse-result risk, preservation matters, and destruction or loss of the body can remove the opportunity. ([S5](https://www.rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Resurrector_mech_serum))

Transferable boundary:

```text
actual death
  -> persisted body and restoration eligibility
  -> recovery, transport, preservation, access, and decision
  -> successful restoration OR eligibility closure
  -> final death transaction only after closure
```

This applies only if a future Stakes authority permits resurrection. It conflicts with the currently accepted restricted-Stakes rule that actual death becomes terminal when committed. Applying it there would require explicit supersession. Ordinary healing must remain separate from resurrection.

Rarity is clearest when eligibility is deterministic but access is scarce: explicit capability, valid body, preservation/time posture, recovery/transport, rare resource or institution, and visible risks. Randomly deciding after death whether resurrection “exists” would feel arbitrary.

## 10. Succession, Legacy, And Reward Integrity

Wildermyth separates death, survival with maiming, and retirement; surviving or retired heroes enter Legacy through campaign completion, while some dead heroes enter through a tomb or final-battle rule. Promotion is completion-gated, and repeating campaign completion from a save does not grant extra promotions because spent promotions are tracked. ([S8](https://wildermyth.com/wiki/Maim), [S9](https://wildermyth.com/wiki/Legacy_tier))

CK3 treats ruler death as a handoff: the dynasty, inherited facts, and world continue under a playable heir. ([S10](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/crusader-kings-iii/about))

For Lineage, irreversible account rewards, estate transfer, terminal Chronicle closure, and heir control should settle only at final death, not at potentially reversible actual death. Any earlier notices or projections must remain provisional and nonpaying. Settlement needs stable source ids and consumed evidence so checkpoint replay cannot duplicate account-side value.

## 11. Player-Communication Findings

Effective comparison patterns expose the decision the player must make:

- KCD names the saving resource and marks eligible beds.
- RimWorld exposes downed actors and allows explicit tend/carry/rescue/capture actions.
- Kenshi exposes localized injury, bleeding, unconsciousness/recovery, and the need for aid.
- Wildermyth presents a mortal choice at the moment of zero HP.
- CK3 frames death as lineage continuation.

Lineage should separately communicate current functional state, active lethal processes, what basic stabilization changes, definitive-care requirements, rescue contributors/blockers, body location and condition, resurrection eligibility, checkpoint provenance, rollback consequences, and the exact moment final closure becomes irreversible.

## 12. Directly Transferable Principles

- Separate chosen rollback, hidden technical recovery, and deterministic outcome commitment.
- Give a causal event stable outcome identity across materially identical replay.
- Derive urgency from function, lethal processes, and care requirements.
- Make first aid process-specific; stabilization is not full recovery.
- Resolve rescue from visible contextual facts and explicit owner rules.
- Persist body and eligibility truth when resurrection is possible.
- Settle terminal rewards and heir control only once finality is authoritative.
- Make all account-side settlements idempotent.

## 13. Adaptable Patterns

- Qualifying major sleep as a manually loadable checkpoint.
- A derived, prominently displayed `stable aid-required` / `unstable` classification.
- Explicit carry/extraction actions and institution-owned recovery destinations.
- Rare resurrection through explicit capability and body eligibility.
- Completion-gated Legacy promotion with spent-entitlement tracking.
- Three Stakes choices only if each has distinct load, commitment, finality, reward, and warning contracts.

## 14. Cautions And Anti-Patterns

- Save scarcity without event commitment still permits repeated RNG fishing.
- A global seeded sequence may invite unrelated action-order manipulation.
- A separately mutable stable-critical state can duplicate injury/process truth.
- One universal bleed-out timer cannot explain bleeding, poison, exposure, suffocation, or continued attack.
- One opaque rescue roll hides causal counterplay.
- Random post-death access to resurrection makes rarity feel arbitrary.
- Paying Prestige or creating an heir at reversible death invites duplication and contradiction.
- Copying exact timers, probabilities, injuries, or proprietary terminology is not justified.

## 15. Bounded Answers To The Ten Questions

1. **Sleep checkpoints or deterministic commitment?** Both are needed if bounded rollback and anti-fishing are goals. Checkpoints bound rewind; event commitment prevents materially identical rerolls.
2. **Hidden crash recovery?** Yes. It should restore the latest verified state and remain nonselectable.
3. **Stable aid-required critical state?** Explicit in presentation, derived in authority from functional state, lethal processes, and care requirement.
4. **Basic stabilization versus recovery?** Yes. Basic aid may stop a named lethal process while definitive care remains necessary for consciousness, function, or healing.
5. **Separate lethal timers?** Use independent process progression where time matters, not one universal timer. The UI may foreground the most urgent process.
6. **Rescue inputs?** Threat intent, active hazards, able helpers, loyalty/permission, skill, supplies, carrying/transport, route, terrain/weather, destination/institution, travel time, and body state. Use rules, not an omnibus roll.
7. **Actual death to final death?** In a resurrection-permitting tier, finality follows eligibility exhaustion, impossibility, or an explicit informed abandonment/closure transaction. Current restricted Stakes instead closes at committed actual death unless superseded.
8. **Rare but fair resurrection?** Make eligibility deterministic and surfaced; make capability, body recovery, preservation, access, resources, institutions, and risks scarce.
9. **Rollback provenance and Prestige?** Tie events and settlements to checkpoint/source ids. Do not durably pay for a death that can be rolled back. Settle final rewards once at a non-reversible boundary; exact tier modifiers remain open.
10. **Three public Stakes tiers?** Potentially yes, but only if broad rollback, checkpoint rollback with committed events/provenance, and no chosen rollback each have distinct enforceable contracts. If the middle tier is merely fewer saves, two choices are clearer.
